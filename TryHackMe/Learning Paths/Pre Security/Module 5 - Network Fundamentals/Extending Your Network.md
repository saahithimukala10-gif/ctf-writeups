# Extending Your Network

**Module:** Network Fundamentals

## Key Points

- Real networks are built by connecting multiple LANs together, and connecting LANs to the wider internet — this is where WANs and extra networking devices come in.
- **WAN (Wide Area Network):** connects LANs across larger distances (cities, countries) — the internet is the largest WAN.
- Key devices for extending/connecting networks:
  - **Router:** connects separate networks (e.g. a home LAN to the ISP/internet) and routes traffic between them based on IP.
  - **Modem:** converts signals between the ISP's medium (cable/fibre/DSL) and a format the router/network can use.
  - **Access Point (AP):** extends a wired network's reach wirelessly (Wi-Fi).
- **NAT (Network Address Translation):** lets many devices on a private LAN share one public IP address when talking to the internet.
- **Firewalls** sit at network boundaries to filter traffic in/out based on rules.

## Remember

LAN = local, WAN = wide-area (internet = biggest WAN). Router connects networks together; modem talks to the ISP; NAT lets a whole LAN share one public IP.
